# Modelling plan: decisions made before feature extraction

This document records the decisions made before beginning feature extraction, following the same discipline used throughout preprocessing: each decision is stated with its reasoning and its sources, distinguishing what's grounded in a specific citation from what's a project-level judgment call.

## 1. Primary dataset variant: heog_off

VEOG correction is validated to a high standard (isolated blink-shaped segments returning to a quiet baseline; correction output shows the expected frontal-to-posterior contamination gradient by channel). HEOG correction, despite genuine effort (duration-bound tightening, literature-grounded bounds, baseline-drift removal validated via a window-size sweep), still shows roughly half of detected segments displaying step-shift/trend character rather than isolated events, not clearly wrong, but not clearly right either, and undistinguished by further available evidence. A known implementation gap also remains: if HEOG correction were enabled, regression would run against non-detrended HEOG data, inconsistent with the detrended version used for detection. Given this, applying an uncertain correction risks subtracting real EEG signal using misidentified noise as the regressor, an active distortion. Leaving it uncorrected retains existing, unremarkable contamination at the level any imperfectly-corrected EEG dataset already has. Between those, not correcting was judged the lower-risk default. This was not treated as a reason to drop the question: the full cohort was run twice (heog_off and heog_on, both fully preprocessed and saved), specifically so the on/off comparison can be tested empirically at the modelling stage rather than assumed. heog_off is primary for all main analyses; heog_on is the dedicated comparison arm for a reported sensitivity check.

## 2. Frequency bands, connectivity metric(s), and power transform

**Bands.** No single authoritative band-boundary table exists in the EEG literature. Newson and Thiagarajan's (2019) review of resting-state EEG studies in psychiatric research exists specifically because definitions vary substantially across the field, and reported ranges commonly span delta 1 to 4 Hz, theta 4 to 8 Hz, alpha 8 to 13 Hz, beta 13 to 30 Hz, with gamma anywhere from 30 to 45 Hz to 30 to 100 Hz depending on the study. Given no standard to defer to, bands were matched to Chang et al. (2025): delta 2 to 4 Hz, theta 4 to 8 Hz, alpha 8 to 13 Hz, beta 13 to 30 Hz, gamma 30 to 45 Hz, the closest direct comparator, since it addresses the same prediction problem (pre-treatment resting EEG predicting rTMS response in MDD) on a comparable sample size (n=94 after exclusions). This is a judgment call justified by comparability to the nearest published benchmark, not a claim that this table is the field standard.

**Connectivity metric.** PLI is primary, with coherence and PLV as secondary comparisons. This is grounded in a specific mechanism, not convention: Stam, Nolte, and Daffertshofer (2007) introduced PLI to address volume conduction and reference-electrode bias in EEG connectivity estimation, and found it performed at least as well as conventional phase coherence at detecting genuine synchronization changes while being substantially less affected by common-source contamination. The mechanism is that PLI and the imaginary part of coherency both discard zero-lag phase relationships, on the reasoning that instantaneous (zero-lag) synchrony between electrodes is more likely to reflect the same underlying source picked up at two sensors than genuine inter-regional coupling, whereas plain coherence and standard PLV can be artificially inflated between spatially close electrodes for exactly this reason. Running coherence/PLV as secondary comparisons alongside PLI gives a direct empirical check on how much of any connectivity-based predictive signal survives volume-conduction correction, mirroring the heog_off/heog_on sensitivity-check structure already used for preprocessing. Caveat for the record: PLI has been reported to have lower test-retest reliability than some PLV variants, so volume-conduction robustness and measurement reliability are separate properties.

**Power transform.** Band power and ratio power features are log-transformed; PLI, PLV, and coherence features are not. The reason for the split: log-normality evidence (Box-Cox optimization studies on resting alpha power, and general findings on EEG power-spectrum skewness) applies to unbounded, right-skewed spectral power. It doesn't apply to PLI/PLV/coherence, which are bounded between 0 and 1 and don't share that skewed shape. Log-transforming a bounded [0,1] metric is also risky in practice, since values near zero blow up toward negative infinity, and these connectivity metrics can legitimately sit near zero for unconnected channel pairs. The benefit of the transform also depends on the model: LDA assumes normally-distributed features within each class, so log power directly fixes a violated assumption there. Regularized linear models (logistic regression, linear SVM) benefit less formally, but still gain more even-handed regularization once features are on comparable scales. Tree ensembles are unaffected either way, since they're invariant to monotonic transforms. As with the planned age-confound regression, this transform will be fit on training folds only at the modelling stage, not applied to the full feature matrix beforehand.

## 3. Epoch-to-subject aggregation and minimum epoch-count threshold

Subjects are aggregated by simple mean across retained epochs, per feature, per subject. This is supported unevenly across feature types rather than uniformly. Dominicus et al. (2025) assessed how strongly three classes of resting-state EEG metrics, functional connectivity (amplitude-envelope correlation and PLI), signal complexity (permutation entropy, PE), and network topology (minimum spanning trees), depend on time and state, and found that power spectral characteristics show high temporal stability, while functional connectivity measures are considerably more variable, with stability decreasing further as retest interval increases from hours to days to a month or longer. The same study found alpha and theta PE, and alpha PLI specifically, to be the most robust measures suitable for biomarker development, with other connectivity metrics requiring caution due to limited stability. This is a second, independent line of evidence supporting PLI as the primary connectivity metric (Decision 2), beyond its volume-conduction robustness. Plain mean aggregation is treated as an adequate default for power features and a known limitation for connectivity features, documented as such rather than resolved with a different aggregation scheme, since Dominicus et al. (2025) do not propose or test a superior alternative for this case.

The minimum epoch-count inclusion threshold is set at approximately 10 epochs (50 seconds of retained resting-state data), anchored to Wiesman et al. (2022), who found that spectral estimate stability in resting-state MEG can be robustly assessed from segments of roughly 30 to 120 seconds, depending on cortical region. Strijbis et al. (2022), citing Wiesman et al., adopted a working floor of 5 epochs of 13 seconds (65 seconds total) on that basis. Translated to this project's 5-second epochs, the Wiesman et al. range corresponds to roughly 6 to 24 epochs. This transfer is treated explicitly as an approximation, not a precise cutoff: Wiesman et al.'s evidence is MEG source-level spectral stability, not EEG sensor-level connectivity, so it anchors the floor rather than proving it. The cohort's retained-epoch range (10 to 24 per subject) sits mostly within this band; subjects at the low end (10 epochs) are flagged as marginal rather than treated as clearly safe.

Retained-epoch-count was checked for correlation with age (n=160, restEC/heog_off, excluding the 3 subjects with missing BDF files): Spearman rho = -0.079 (p = 0.322), Pearson r = -0.116 (p = 0.145). Neither is significant. The floor does not introduce
an age-related selection effect on top of the already-documented age confound (Decision 7).

In practice, the floor is non-binding for this cohort: retained epochs range from 21 to 24, well above the 10-epoch threshold, with no subjects falling below 15. The floor remains a necessary methodological decision to state and justify, but no subjects in this cohort are actually excluded or marginal because of it.

## 4. Resting condition: restEC primary, restEO as sensitivity arm

restEC is primary; restEO is the documented comparison arm, structured the same way as the heog_off/heog_on split (Decision 1).

No literature consensus favors either condition for this specific prediction task. Duan et al. (2021) found slightly higher general reproducibility for eyes-closed rsEEG. Alpha-band signal, relevant given the alpha-based biomarkers in the candidate feature literature, is substantially attenuated in eyes-open recordings (van Dijk et al., 2022, d = 0.89).

The pilot preprocessing notebook raised a concrete concern: one subject had zero valid VEOG segments in restEC, risking uncalibrated ocular correction if this generalized. Checked against the 6-subject batch test: it did not generalize. Zero subjects had zero valid VEOG segments in either condition, and restEC in fact produced more candidate and valid VEOG segments on average than restEO (42.5 vs. 27.8 candidates; 37.8 vs. 25.8 valid). Epoch retention was identical across conditions (median 0.958). HEOG candidate counts were lower and exclusion rates higher in restEC, but this doesn't affect the primary pipeline since HEOG correction is off in heog_off.

## 5. Classifier family and dimensionality strategy

Three primary classifiers are compared on a literature-curated, replication-weighted candidate feature pool: Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage LDA, elastic-net regularized logistic regression, and Bayesian logistic regression with a regularizing prior.

The exclusion of tree ensembles from this primary set is grounded in a direct comparator, not a general small-sample worry. Chang et al. (2025), working on the same prediction problem, found XGBoost's cross-validated accuracy fell from 84.11% to a mean of 63.79% (SD 4.61%) across 1,000 shuffles at n=75, while LDA held stable at 86.36%. Varoquaux (2018) explains why this instability is expected at these sample sizes: cross-validation error bars are substantially underestimated relative to their true variability once sample size drops into this range.

Standard LDA's requirement to invert a feature covariance matrix breaks down once feature count approaches sample count. Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage (Ledoit & Wolf, 2004) addresses this directly, and Lotfi, Shahsavani, and Arashi (2022) found it outperforms standard LDA specifically in high-dimensional classification settings comparable to this project's feature-to-sample ratio. This removes the need for Chang et al.'s external two-stage feature reduction (RFE-SVC followed by sequential backward selection) as a workaround.

Bayesian logistic regression with a regularizing prior is included as a third primary candidate rather than as an exploratory addition, since the prior performs the same shrinkage function as elastic-net's penalty term, making it a small-sample-appropriate method by design rather than a small-sample liability.

The dimensionality strategy for the primary arm is a candidate feature pool curated from prior rTMS-EEG biomarker literature, weighted explicitly by replication status. Widge et al. (2019) found no single QEEG biomarker outperformed the pooled meta-analytic estimate and concluded QEEG is not yet clinically reliable, citing publication bias and insufficient replication. The ICON-DB consortium's direct replication attempts sharpen this further: individual alpha frequency (IAF) proximity to 10 Hz (Corlier et al., 2019) was independently replicated (Roelofs et al., 2021), but only for the 10 Hz unilateral left-DLPFC rTMS subgroup, not for 1 Hz or bilateral protocols in the same replication study. Frontal theta connectivity and resting alpha power (Bailey et al., 2018) failed to replicate in a dedicated ICON-DB attempt (Bailey et al., 2020). Frontal alpha asymmetry was flagged as likely unreliable by van der Vinne et al. (2017), cited directly in the TDBRAIN paper's own introduction.

IAF-proximity is excluded from the primary feature pool despite being the best-replicated single candidate in this literature (Corlier et al., 2019; independently replicated by Roelofs et al., 2021), because its evidence base only covers the 10Hz unilateral left-DLPFC subgroup - protocol 1 in this cohort, n=42 of 163 (26%). Applying it across the full cohort would mean 74% of subjects have no literature basis for what the feature should represent; imputing or otherwise extending it to those subjects would not be a faithful use of the replicated finding.

Instead, IAF-proximity is reported as a dedicated supplementary analysis, restricted to the n=42 protocol-1 subjects, structured the same way as the heog_off/heog_on and restEC/restEO comparison arms (Decisions 1 and 4): a pre-specified check reported regardless of outcome, not folded into the primary model's feature set. At n=42, this analysis is explicitly underpowered as a standalone classifier and is not expected to produce a reliable accuracy estimate on its own, its value is in testing whether the replicated association holds in this specific cohort, not in contributing to primary model
performance.

Frontal theta connectivity and alpha power remain in the primary pool as documented failed-replication checks (Bailey et al., 2018; non-replication in Bailey et al., 2020), not expected predictors. Frontal alpha asymmetry remains deprioritized.

**Protocol 3 discrepancy (unresolved).** van Dijk et al. (2022), Table 2, reports only 7 MDD subjects on rTMS protocol 3 across the full 1274-subject database. This cohort's V3.1 release shows 32 subjects coded protocol 3 (of 163) — confirmed not a denominator artefact (all 163 subjects have complete BDI pre/post; filtering to BDI-complete subjects does not change the count). The cause is unresolved: possibly dataset growth or recoding between the 2022 publication and the V3.1 release, or a difference in what the `rTMS PROTOCOL` field represents between versions. This is flagged as an open item rather than resolved, and is a candidate for direct follow-up with Brainclinics if it becomes material to the modelling results.

A secondary, exploratory arm uses the full feature bank (all channels, all bands, all connectivity metrics, roughly 1,755+ features before coherence and PLV are added), with nested feature selection inside CV per Shim, Lee, and Hwang (2021). XGBoost and random forest are tested only in this secondary arm, specifically to check whether nonlinear flexibility captures signal the primary linear/shrinkage models miss; instability here, consistent with the Chang et al. finding, is an expected and reportable outcome, not a failure to avoid.

Both arms are pre-specified together, before either is run, and both are reported in full regardless of outcome. This avoids the arms becoming an implicit multiple-comparisons problem where only the better-performing one gets reported.

## 6. Kuramoto synchrony metrics

Order parameter and metastability, computed per epoch using the same aggregation approach as the spectral and connectivity features, as a secondary comparison against the spectral/connectivity baseline. This matches the project's stated framing: does synchrony add predictive value beyond standard features, not synchrony as a primary claim on its own. Full coupling-matrix fitting remains explicitly out of scope, given sample size constraints. Exact channel grouping and frequency band are to be finalized when this feature family is implemented.

## 7. Age confound handling

Deferred to the modelling stage, not built into feature extraction. Age is regressed out of features within training folds only, per this project's existing documented plan, avoiding train/test leakage. Feature extraction computes raw features; age-adjustment is a fold-scoped transform applied afterward.
