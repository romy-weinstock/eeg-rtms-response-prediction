# Modelling plan: decisions made before feature extraction 
This document records the decisions made before beginning feature extraction, following the same discipline used throughout preprocessing: each decision is stated with its reasoning and its sources, distinguishing what's grounded in a specific citation from what's a project-level judgment call.
## 1. Primary dataset variant: 
heog_off VEOG correction is validated to a high standard (isolated blink-shaped segments returning to a quiet baseline; correction output shows the expected frontal-to-posterior contamination gradient by channel). HEOG correction, despite genuine effort (duration-bound tightening, literature-grounded bounds, baseline-drift removal validated via a window-size sweep), still shows roughly half of detected segments displaying step-shift/trend character rather than isolated events - not clearly wrong, but not clearly right either, and undistinguished by further available evidence. A known implementation gap also remains: if HEOG correction were enabled, regression would run against non-detrended HEOG data, inconsistent with the detrended version used for detection. Given this, applying an uncertain correction risks subtracting real EEG signal using misidentified noise as the regressor - an active distortion. Leaving it uncorrected retains existing, unremarkable contamination at the level any imperfectly-corrected EEG dataset already has. Between those, not correcting was judged the lower-risk default. This was **not treated as a reason to drop the question**: the full cohort was run twice (heog_off and heog_on, both fully preprocessed and saved), specifically so the on/off comparison can be tested empirically at the modelling stage rather than assumed. heog_off is primary for all main analyses; heog_on is the dedicated comparison arm for a reported sensitivity check.
## 2. Frequency bands, connectivity metric(s), and power transform
**Bands**. No single authoritative band-boundary table exists in the EEG literature - Newson & Thiagarajan's (2019) review of 184 psychiatric resting-state EEG studies exists specifically because definitions vary substantially across the field, and reported ranges commonly span delta 1-4 Hz, theta 4-8 Hz, alpha 8-13 Hz, beta 13-30 Hz, with gamma anywhere from 30-45 Hz to 30-100 Hz depending on the study. Given no standard to defer to, bands were matched to Chang et al. (2025) - delta 2-4 Hz, theta 4-8 Hz, alpha 8-13 Hz, beta 13-30 Hz, gamma 30-45 Hz - the closest direct comparator, since it addresses the same prediction problem (pre-treatment resting EEG predicting rTMS response in MDD) on a comparable sample size (n=94 after exclusions). This is a judgment call justified by comparability to the nearest published benchmark, not a claim that this table is the field standard.

**Connectivity metric**. PLI is primary, with coherence and PLV as secondary comparisons. This is grounded in a specific mechanism, not convention: Stam, Nolte & Daffertshofer (2007) introduced PLI to address volume conduction and reference-electrode bias in EEG connectivity estimation, and found it performed at least as well as conventional phase coherence at detecting genuine synchronization changes while being substantially less affected by common-source contamination. The mechanism is that PLI and the imaginary part of coherency both discard zero-lag phase relationships, on the reasoning that instantaneous (zero-lag) synchrony between electrodes is more likely to reflect the same underlying source picked up at two sensors than genuine inter-regional coupling - whereas plain coherence and standard PLV can be artificially inflated between spatially close electrodes for exactly this reason. Running coherence/PLV as secondary comparisons alongside PLI gives a direct empirical check on how much of any connectivity-based predictive signal survives volume-conduction correction, mirroring the heog_off/heog_on sensitivity-check structure already used for preprocessing. Caveat for the record: PLI has been reported to have lower test-retest reliability than some PLV variants, so volume-conduction robustness and measurement reliability are separate properties.

**Power transform**. Band power and ratio power features are log-transformed; PLI, PLV, and coherence features are not. The reason for the split: log-normality evidence (Box-Cox optimization studies on resting alpha power, and general findings on EEG power-spectrum skewness) applies to unbounded, right-skewed spectral power. It doesn't apply to PLI/PLV/coherence, which are bounded between 0 and 1 and don't share that skewed shape. Log-transforming a bounded [0,1] metric is also risky in practice, since values near zero blow up toward negative infinity, and these connectivity metrics can legitimately sit near zero for unconnected channel pairs. The benefit of the transform also depends on the model: LDA assumes normally-distributed features within each class, so log power directly fixes a violated assumption there. Regularized linear models (logistic regression, linear SVM) benefit less formally, but still gain more even-handed regularization once features are on comparable scales. Tree ensembles are unaffected either way, since they're invariant to monotonic transforms. As with the planned age-confound regression, this transform will be fit on training folds only at the modelling stage, not applied to the full feature matrix beforehand.

## 3. Epoch-to-subject aggregation and minimum epoch-count threshold

Subjects are aggregated by simple mean across retained epochs, per feature, per subject. This is supported unevenly across feature types rather than uniformly. van Dellen et al. (2025) assessed how strongly three classes of resting-state EEG metrics, functional connectivity (amplitude-envelope correlation and PLI), signal complexity (permutation entropy, PE), and network topology (minimum spanning trees), depend on time and state, and found that power spectral characteristics show high temporal stability, while functional connectivity measures are considerably more variable, with stability decreasing further as retest interval increases from hours to days to a month or longer. The same study found alpha and theta PE, and alpha PLI specifically, to be the most robust measures suitable for biomarker development, with other connectivity metrics requiring caution due to limited stability. This is a second, independent line of evidence supporting PLI as the primary connectivity metric (Decision 2), beyond its volume-conduction robustness. Plain mean aggregation is treated as an adequate default for power features and a known limitation for connectivity features, documented as such rather than resolved with a different aggregation scheme, since van Dellen et al. do not propose or test a superior alternative for this case.

The minimum epoch-count inclusion threshold is set at approximately 10 epochs (50 seconds of retained resting-state data), anchored to Wiesman et al. (2022), who found that spectral estimate stability in resting-state MEG can be robustly assessed from segments of roughly 30 to 120 seconds, depending on cortical region. Strijbis et al. (2022), citing Wiesman et al., adopted a working floor of 5 epochs of 13 seconds (65 seconds total) on that basis. Translated to this project's 5-second epochs, the Wiesman et al. range corresponds to roughly 6 to 24 epochs. This transfer is treated explicitly as an approximation, not a precise cutoff: Wiesman et al.'s evidence is MEG source-level spectral stability, not EEG sensor-level connectivity, so it anchors the floor rather than proving it. The cohort's retained-epoch range (10 to 24 per subject) sits mostly within this band; subjects at the low end (10 epochs) are flagged as marginal rather than treated as clearly safe.

Before this threshold is finalized, retained-epoch-count will be checked for correlation with age. This is a project-specific empirical step, not something resolved by the cited studies: if a uniform epoch-count floor correlates with age (e.g., due to systematically higher artifact rates in a particular age group), applying it uniformly could introduce an age-related selection effect on top of the already-documented age confound (Decision 7), rather than being a neutral quality-control step.
